\section{Research Experience}

\row{\textbf{Physics-residual Flow Matching under distribution shift}}%
    {\rg{https://amiteshpuri.github.io/reports/fm-ood-residual.pdf}{https://github.com/AmiteshPuri/fm-ood-residual}}
\row{\textit{Independent research report}}{}
\vspace{1.5pt}

A common argument for physics-constrained generative models is that respecting the governing equation
should keep them reliable when the data moves away from training. This experimental study examined this on a
next-step operator for 2D Navier-Stokes, comparing plain flow matching with a weighted physics residual
and a conflict-free gradient variant across increasing shift severity and sampling budget. In
distribution, the weighted residual lowered relative error by 37~\% and the PDE residual by 44~\% over five seeds, and matched the baseline's 64-step accuracy in 4 to 8 steps, while the
conflict-free variant could not be separated from the baseline on any in-distribution metric. The
advantage did not survive higher-wavenumber initial conditions or stronger forcing. Under both shifts
the residual model degraded about twice as much as the baseline relative to its own in-distribution
error. At the strongest initial-condition shift it was 33~\% less accurate than the baseline, and
under the strongest forcing its rollouts failed at step 9.6 against 15.6. Even with the full governing
equation built into the residual, the gain held only in distribution and within a narrow range of
residual weights. The inductive bias of the residual is only as useful as the physics it encodes, which
makes it unlikely to help where the equation is unknown or only partly specified, as in coupled
multiphysics systems.

\section{Master's Research}

\noindent
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.80\linewidth}
  \raggedright\textbf{Addressing Conditional Shift in Graph Data: An Empirical Study of Distance Metrics}
\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.18\linewidth}
  \raggedleft\rg{https://amiteshpuri.github.io/thesis/MSc_Project_Report.pdf}{https://github.com/AmiteshPuri/MSc--Research-Project}
\end{minipage}\par
\row{\textit{Master Research Report}}{}
\vspace{1.5pt}

GNNs trained on a labelled subset that does not represent the whole graph lose accuracy on the
remaining nodes, a conditional shift between training and full distributions. The project added KLD,
JSD, MMD and CMD to the CE loss as regularisers, alone and in weighted pairs, and used conformal
prediction to measure reliability through prediction sets with a coverage guarantee. Over five
architectures and 15 runs on Cora, every CMD-based regulariser raised accuracy under bias for all
architectures except GAT, by up to 4.3 points. The largest recovery was GraphSAGE with CMD, from 0.692
to 0.736 against 0.808 without bias, which won back 37\% of the accuracy lost to the bias, the
most of any model on either dataset. The same regularisers cut the average prediction set by 30 to 45\%
for all except GCN at the 90\% coverage target, while KLD, JSD and MMD alone stayed near
the CE baseline. CMD alone performed close to the paired versions on most architectures, so it carried
most of the gain, and the effect was weaker on Citeseer. Since these measures only align the distributions of training and test
representations, with no model of what produces the shift, the results suggest that alignment agnostic
to the source of the shift can narrow the generalisation gap but not close it.
