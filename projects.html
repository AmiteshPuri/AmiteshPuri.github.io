Project 01 · MSc research

Addressing Conditional Shift in Graph Data

Graph neural networks · distribution shift · uncertainty

My Master's research report: an empirical study of whether distance-based regularisers that align training and test node representations improve the robustness and calibration of graph neural networks under conditional distribution shift.

Research question

Research question

When the conditional relationship between node features and labels shifts between training and test graphs, can distance-metric regularisation recover accuracy and calibration — and which distance helps most?

Semi-supervised node classification usually assumes training and test nodes are drawn from the same distribution. Under conditional shift, the conditional \(P(Y \mid X)\) differs between the two, and standard training silently degrades. This project treats that degradation as the object of study rather than a nuisance.

Methodology

Across several GNN architectures on benchmark node-classification datasets, a distance term between the training- and test-node representation distributions is added to the task loss as an auxiliary regulariser:

\[ \mathcal{L} \;=\; \mathcal{L}_{\text{task}} \;+\; \lambda \, D\big(Z_{\text{train}},\, Z_{\text{test}}\big) \]

where \(Z\) are learned node representations and \(D(\cdot,\cdot)\) is the distance metric under study. The metric is the one component varied in a controlled way; everything else in the pipeline is held fixed, so differences can be attributed to the choice of \(D\). Uncertainty is quantified with conformal prediction, which supplies coverage guarantees that can be checked directly under distributional change.

Experimental design

  • Several GNN architectures evaluated on benchmark node-classification datasets under induced conditional shift.
  • Controlled ablations isolating each distance metric's contribution to out-of-distribution accuracy, calibration error, and robustness.
  • Conformal prediction used as the uncertainty-quantification method, evaluating empirical coverage under shift.
  • Implemented in PyTorch with fixed random seeds and logged hyperparameters for reproducibility; code kept public.

Contribution & scope

The contribution is empirical: a like-for-like comparison of distance metrics as shift regularisers for GNNs, measured jointly on accuracy, calibration, and conformal coverage rather than accuracy alone. This is Master's-level research, not a publication. Detailed quantitative results are in the report below; they are not reproduced here so that nothing is stated out of context.

Project 02 · Neural operators

Neural Operator Failure Mechanisms Under Distribution Shift

FNO · DeepONet · UNO · Darcy flow · diagnostics

Existing work catalogues that neural operators degrade out of distribution. This project reproduces that multi-seed protocol on Darcy flow, then adds two original diagnostics that probe the internal mechanism behind a failure rather than only its magnitude.

Research question

Research question

Why do neural operators fail under distribution shift — not just how much does error grow, but where in the solution and in the operator's response does the failure originate?

Setup

The task is the Darcy-flow solution operator: given a permeability field \(a(x)\), predict the pressure field \(u(x)\) solving the elliptic PDE

\[ -\nabla\!\cdot\!\big(a(x)\,\nabla u(x)\big) \;=\; f(x). \]

Three operator-learning architectures — a Fourier Neural Operator (FNO), a DeepONet, and a U-shaped Neural Operator (UNO) — are trained across a 50-seed sweep, with permeability fields drawn as Gaussian random fields and reference solutions from a finite-difference solver. Distribution shift is induced through resolution-extrapolation and frequency-shift splits.

Diagnostics, precisely

Spectral failure. Prediction error is radially averaged in Fourier space to see whether a model loses global low-frequency structure or fine-scale detail above a cutoff — the expected failure mode for FNO, whose mode truncation imposes a hard spectral limit:

\[ E(k) \;=\; \big|\hat{u}_{\text{pred}}(k) - \hat{u}_{\text{GT}}(k)\big|, \qquad k = |\mathbf{k}|. \]

Noise robustness. Zero-mean Gaussian noise is added to the normalised permeability input at increasing \(\sigma\), with 50 independent draws per level:

\[ a_{\text{noisy}} \;=\; a \;+\; \sigma\,\mathcal{N}(0,\,1). \]

Error localisation (extension). The pointwise error \(e = |u - \hat{u}|\) is correlated with the solution's spatial structure, testing whether error concentrates near steep gradients or high curvature:

\[ \rho \;=\; \operatorname{Pearson}\!\big(e,\, |\nabla u|\big) \quad\text{and}\quad \operatorname{Pearson}\!\big(e,\, |\nabla^2 u|\big). \]

Sensitivity mismatch (extension). The operator's local response to a perturbation is compared against the solver's, via central differences along a random direction \(v\), reported as cosine similarity and relative error:

\[ S(a, v) \;=\; \frac{u(a + \varepsilon v) - u(a - \varepsilon v)}{2\varepsilon}. \]

A model can match output accuracy on a perturbed input while having an entirely wrong local Jacobian — interpolating between training examples rather than tracking the operator. Output error cannot reveal that; this diagnostic can.

Quantitative results live in the CSV outputs and analysis notebook of the repository, and will be summarised in the report. The figure slots below are reserved for the actual exported plots; nothing is shown until real results are dropped in.

Limitations & scope

This repository implements only Darcy flow — a static, elliptic PDE — so rollout and compounding-error diagnostics do not apply here, since there is no time-stepping to compound. Boundary-condition shift is not implemented; it is the natural next extension if the codebase is generalised to a time-dependent PDE.

Project 03 · Generative modelling

Physics-Informed Latent Generative Modelling for 2D Navier–Stokes

Transformer VQ-VAE · latent flow matching · PDE residual

A two-stage generative surrogate for 2D Navier–Stokes, built to answer one question directly: does adding a physics residual to representation learning — not just to the final predictor — produce better generative models of the flow?

Research question

Research question

Does physics-informed latent representation learning improve generative surrogate modelling of a PDE, compared with an architecturally identical baseline trained without the physics term?

Architecture

Two stages. In Stage 1, a Transformer VQ-VAE compresses velocity-field snapshots into a discrete latent code: ViT-style patch tokenisation, a Transformer encoder, an EMA vector quantiser with dead-code reset, and a Transformer decoder. In Stage 2, a latent flow-matching model learns to generate new codebook embeddings over the frozen Stage-1 codebook, and the frozen decoder turns generated latents back into velocity fields.

The physics term

The governing equations are the 2D incompressible Navier–Stokes equations in vorticity form, which eliminates the pressure term entirely — appropriate here because the model reconstructs velocity, with no pressure channel. With velocity \(\mathbf{u}=(u,v)\) and vorticity \(\omega=\partial_x v-\partial_y u\):

\[ \nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf{u} = 0, \qquad \partial_t \omega + (\mathbf{u}\!\cdot\!\nabla)\,\omega = \nu\,\Delta\omega + f. \]

Stage 1 is trained in two variants. The baseline minimises reconstruction plus vector-quantisation loss; the physics-informed variant adds a scale-normalised residual penalty:

\[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{VQ-VAE}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{VQ}} + w\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{phys}}, \qquad \mathcal{L}_{\text{phys}} = \lambda_1\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{div}} + \lambda_2\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{vort}}. \]

The residual is normalised by the RMS of its leading-order term, so it stays order-one regardless of viscosity or dataset — avoiding the multi-order-of-magnitude weight retuning a raw-MSE physics loss would demand. Baseline versus physics-informed is a single scalar, \(w\); everything else is held identical. Because the VQ-VAE reconstructs a single snapshot, the time-derivative term is estimated from the dataset's own adjacent frames, so the residual measures spatial self-consistency with the true local evolution rather than a closed-loop rollout.

Generation & evaluation

Stage 2 uses flow matching (chosen over latent diffusion) with a DiT-style Transformer vector field and the conditional flow-matching / rectified-flow objective on the frozen codebook embeddings:

\[ \mathbf{x}_t = (1-t)\,\mathbf{x}_0 + t\,\mathbf{x}_1, \qquad \mathcal{L}_{\text{FM}} = \mathbb{E}\,\big\|\, v_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t) - (\mathbf{x}_1 - \mathbf{x}_0)\,\big\|^2. \]

Generation integrates the learned ODE from Gaussian noise and snaps the result to the nearest codebook vector before decoding. Generated fields are evaluated with maximum mean discrepancy, Wasserstein distance, spectral energy error, and PDE residual. One honest caveat is documented in the code: an unconditionally generated snapshot has no true next timestep, so its residual pairs the generated field with a randomly drawn real context — it tests whether generated fields have plausible lengthscales and gradients, not closed-loop rollout consistency.

Results

The comparison between baseline and physics-informed representations is the point of the project; results are produced from checkpoints and logs by the analysis notebook. These slots are reserved for the actual figures.

Limitations & scope

The physics residual is snapshot-level, not a full space-time residual, and the default experiments run on a synthetic pseudospectral Navier–Stokes solver rather than a substitute for real data. The generated-field residual is a plausibility measure, not a rollout-consistency guarantee. These choices are documented in the repository so the numbers are not over-interpreted.

Project 04 · Representation learning

Spectral vs Patch-Based Tokenization of PDE Fields

Representation learning · in progress

A representation-learning study of a question that sits underneath the generative work: how should a continuous PDE solution field be turned into tokens for a downstream model in the first place?

Active project — repository and report to be released

Research question

Research question

How should continuous PDE solution fields be represented and tokenized for downstream generative modelling — and does a patch-based, spectral, or hybrid representation serve that goal best?

Why field tokenization is its own problem

Tokenizing a scientific field is not the same problem as tokenizing language. A solution field has spatial locality, multiscale structure, and continuity; it carries genuine spectral content and physical invariants; and its meaning depends on the discretisation resolution. A representation that ignores these can throw away exactly the structure a physical model needs.

Approach

The plan is a controlled comparison: patch-based (ViT-style) tokenization, spectral (FNO-style) representation, and a hybrid, evaluated on the same downstream task with shared components identified so that any difference is attributable to the representation rather than the rest of the pipeline. This project is in progress; results, the report, and the repository will be linked here as they become available. No results are shown until they exist.
