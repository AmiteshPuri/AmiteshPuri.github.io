Research

The direction behind the projects

This page is about the ideas connecting my work, not a list of it. The projects are the evidence; here is the question they are all circling.

Motivation

Many physical systems are described by partial differential equations — compact statements that carry strong structure: conservation laws, symmetries, characteristic scales, and a geometry the solution lives on. Machine learning is very good at fitting such data and much less careful about keeping that structure intact. A learned model of a fluid can look right and still violate incompressibility; a learned solution operator can be accurate in-distribution and quietly wrong the moment resolution or parameters move.

The question I keep returning to:

How can machine learning build useful representations and generative models of complex physical systems while retaining the mathematical and structural properties of those systems — and staying reliable under changes in resolution, parameters, and distribution?

Written abstractly, the object is a system \(\partial_t u = \mathcal{N}(u)\) with a solution operator \(\mathcal{G}: a \mapsto u\) mapping parameters or inputs to solutions. The goal is not only to approximate \(\mathcal{G}\), but to do so in a representation that keeps the constraints \(u\) must satisfy — and to know when the approximation stops holding. I treat this as open, not solved.

Long-term goals

Where this is going

My long-term goal is to develop mathematically grounded machine-learning methods for scientific computing: generative and representation-learning approaches that capture the structure of complex dynamical systems while remaining reliable under changes in resolution, parameters, and distribution.

The three current projects map onto the same programme from different sides. The neural-operator work asks when learned solution operators can be trusted out of distribution. The physics-informed latent generative work asks whether building physical structure into a representation yields better generative surrogates. The tokenization work asks what representation of a continuous field makes any of this tractable. My MSc research on distribution shift is where the reliability thread started.

I would like to pursue this in a PhD — deepening the mathematics, widening the class of systems, and treating reliability as a first-class requirement rather than an afterthought. I do not claim to have solved any of this; I have built the first pieces and want to keep going.
